    Eat the rainbow

    Five colours to aim for

    Different coloured fruit and vegetables bring different flavours and textures to the plate. Try to include a few colours across the day.

    Red

    Tomatoes, peppers, strawberries, radishes, red onion.

    Orange

    Carrots, squash, sweet potatoes, oranges, mangoes.

    Yellow

    Corn, yellow peppers, lemons, pineapple, bananas.

    Green

    Spinach, broccoli, peas, courgettes, herbs, kiwis.

    Purple

    Red cabbage, aubergine, beetroot, blueberries, plums.

    Waste less

    Use up every last bit

    • Soft tomatoes make great sauces and soups.
    • Wilting herbs blend into pesto or herb oil.
    • Overripe bananas are perfect for baking and smoothies.
    • Vegetable peelings can be simmered into stock.
    • Stale bread becomes croutons for tomorrow’s salad.
    Fresh habits

    Small habits, big flavour

    • Dress salads at the last minute so they stay crisp.
    • Season with acid: a squeeze of lemon brightens almost anything.
    • Add herbs at the end for the freshest flavour.
    • Shop twice a week for delicate produce like berries and leaves.

    Questions

    Fresh food FAQ

    How do I keep salad leaves fresh for longer?

    Wash and dry them thoroughly, then store in a container lined with a paper towel in the salad drawer. Excess moisture is what makes leaves go slimy.

    Should I wash fruit and vegetables before storing them?

    It’s usually best to wash produce just before you eat or cook it. Washing before storing adds moisture, which can speed up spoilage, especially for berries and mushrooms.

    How can I ripen an avocado faster?

    Put it in a paper bag with a banana or apple at room temperature. These fruits release ethylene gas, which speeds up ripening. Once ripe, keep it in the fridge.

    Are frozen fruit and vegetables as good as fresh?

    They’re a great option. Most are frozen soon after harvest, so they keep their colour and flavour well. They’re ideal for smoothies, soups, stir-fries and sauces.

    What’s the easiest way to eat more vegetables?

    Add one extra vegetable to meals you already make: spinach in pasta, grated carrot in sauces, a side salad with dinner or vegetable sticks with lunch.
