The fresh produce guide

How to choose, wash and store fruit and vegetables so they stay fresh for longer, plus what to look for through the seasons.

Choosing

How to pick the best produce

  • Use your senses: look for bright colour, firm texture and a fresh smell.
  • Feel the weight: citrus, melons and tomatoes should feel heavy for their size.
  • Check the stems and leaves: they should look perky, not wilted.
  • Buy for the week: pick some ripe produce for now and firmer pieces for later.
Washing

Washing fruit and vegetables

  • Rinse produce under cool running water just before eating or cooking.
  • Scrub firm produce like potatoes and carrots with a clean brush.
  • No soap or detergent is needed; water is enough.
  • Dry leaves well in a salad spinner or clean towel.
  • Wash hands and boards after handling raw meat before preparing produce.
Storing

Where and how long to store

ProduceBest placeKeeps for
Leafy greensFridge, salad drawer, in a lined container3–5 days
BerriesFridge, unwashed, in a single layer2–3 days
TomatoesRoom temperature, away from sun3–5 days once ripe
BananasRoom temperature, away from other fruit3–5 days
ApplesFridge for longest life3–4 weeks
CarrotsFridge, tops removed3–4 weeks
PotatoesCool, dark, dry place; not the fridge2–3 weeks
OnionsCool, dry, well-ventilated place1–2 months
Fresh herbsStems in water, loosely covered5–7 days
AvocadosCounter to ripen, then fridge2–3 days once ripe
CitrusFridge for longest life2–3 weeks
MushroomsFridge, in a paper bag4–6 days

Times are typical and depend on ripeness when bought. Keep ethylene producers such as bananas, apples and avocados away from leafy greens.

Seasons

What’s fresh through the year

Spring

Asparagus, peas, radishes, spinach, spring onions, strawberries.

Summer

Tomatoes, courgettes, corn, peppers, berries, peaches, melons.

Autumn

Apples, pears, squash, mushrooms, beetroot, grapes.

Winter

Citrus, cabbage, kale, leeks, parsnips, sweet potatoes.
